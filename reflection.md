A3. Comparison - What Was Similar? What Was Different?

Both AI models appreciated our choice of topic, the explanation of AlertZero and our discussion of the benefits and risks of AI.



Both models suggested completing the AI evaluations and reflection document.



However, their suggested grades were different.

A4. Do You Agree With the AI's Grade?

We partially agree with the suggested grades.



Both AI models correctly identified that our evaluation and reflection files were unfinished at the time of evaluation.



However, we do not completely agree with the GitHub collaboration score given by Gemini.



Our group used branches, commits, Issues, Pull Requests and Discussions throughout the project.



Gemini could not access this work, which affected its suggested grade.



Similarly, ChatGPT could not properly verify our video and deducted marks for that reason.



We believe both grades were affected by the limitations of the AI models rather than the quality of our work alone.

A5. What Would You Do Differently?

If we completed this project again, we would provide more supporting evidence to both AI models during the evaluation.



For example, we could provide screenshots of our GitHub commits, Pull Requests and Issues if an AI could not access our repository.



We would also provide the presentation script if an AI could not review our video.



We would check the video duration more carefully and verify all research sources before submission.



We would not change our research topic or discussion question simply because the AI models gave us lower grades.



This experience taught us that AI feedback can be useful, but it should not replace human judgment.

Part B - AI Research Assistance Reflection

B1. How You Used AI During Research

We used Google Gemini and ChatGPT during our project.



Gemini helped us understand Elastic AlertZero, its purpose and its role in cybersecurity.



ChatGPT helped us organize our research, prepare presentation scripts, create slides and understand the GitHub workflow.



We also used research articles and official documentation to support our information instead of relying entirely on AI-generated answers.

B2. What Worked Well

AI helped us understand technical information in simpler language.



For example, we asked Gemini to explain the background and purpose of Elastic AlertZero.



It helped us understand how AI workers can assist Security Operations Centers with security alerts and investigations.



ChatGPT also helped us organize our research into presentation sections, making it easier for each team member to understand their responsibilities.



This saved time and helped us organize our project.

B3. What Did Not Work Well

AI was not always able to access all the information needed to evaluate our project.



For example, Gemini could not access our GitHub repository and gave us only 5 out of 25 for GitHub collaboration, even though our group had completed many GitHub tasks.



ChatGPT could access parts of our repository but could not properly verify our video.



During presentation preparation, we also noticed that ChatGPT introduced additional research references. We needed to check these references and make sure they supported our topic.



These experiences showed us that AI-generated information should not be accepted without checking its accuracy.

B4. Overall Reflection

Using AI helped our group understand technical concepts, organize tasks and prepare our presentation more efficiently.



However, we learned that AI has limitations and cannot always access or evaluate all the available information.



If we completed a similar project again, we would continue using AI for research assistance and organization, but we would verify its answers more carefully.



The most important lesson we learned is that AI is a helpful tool, but human judgment and critical thinking are still necessary.
